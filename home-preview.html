It starts with a full diagnostic

Most lost points aren’t about what a student doesn’t know.

The diagnostic is a full-length, timed digital SAT, taken at home on the same kind of screen as test day. While your student works, the platform records how every question was answered: how long it took, whether the answer changed, what was flagged, and whether they stepped away.

That is where strong students lose their points. A student who knows the material can still give away a handful of questions to the clock and to second-guessing, and a score alone will never show it. The report finds exactly where it happened, so we fix the habit instead of re-teaching what they already know.

The course

The SAT asks the same questions in a hundred disguises.

The test returns to a small set of core concepts again and again. So the course doesn’t skim a long list of topics. It takes each concept the SAT tests most and develops it properly, lesson after lesson, until your student recognizes every version of it on sight. Linear equations alone gets four lessons and thirteen parts.

  • Know the question typeEvery lesson starts from how the SAT actually asks it: the wording, the setups and the variations, so the question is recognized the moment it appears.
  • Know where the wrong answers come fromEach wrong choice is built from a predictable mistake. Students learn those traps by name, so they stop walking into them.
  • One reliable approach, every timeA method that works on every version of the question, practiced on real SAT questions until it is automatic, then checked before moving on.

Why this works

Most programs are built for the middle of the scale.

Above 1400, the points that remain are few, specific and different for every student. Finding them takes a closer read than a standard course can give.

Practice on your own

Official practice tests are excellent, and your student will take them with me. But a score report can’t tell you whether a miss was a gap, a rushed minute or a second guess, and that is exactly what decides the next 50 points.

Questions

What parents usually ask.

What does it cost?
Sessions are billed by the hour, individually or from a prepaid pack, and how many your student needs depends on where they start and where they are aiming. So you see the plan and the total in writing before anything is scheduled. The diagnostic is free, and the report is yours either way. The payment terms are in the terms.
Why one tutor instead of a company?
Every session is with me. The person who reads your student’s data is the person who teaches them, every week. There are no handoffs to junior tutors and no rotating staff.
Online or in person?
Online, or in person at the Cranford Public Library. Same sessions, same materials, same portal, and many families mix the two.
When should we start?
Ideally three to four months before the test. Juniors usually start once PSAT scores come back, aiming for a March or May SAT. After the diagnostic I’ll give you a realistic timeline for your student.
Does the PSAT matter for National Merit?
For strong sophomores and juniors, yes. National Merit eligibility comes from the junior-year PSAT, and New Jersey’s cutoff is one of the highest in the country, so a student near it should prepare for the October PSAT as seriously as for the SAT.
How will I know it’s working?
You get an update after every session: what we worked on, how the practice went, and any new score. Every practice test lands in the portal with the same full report as the diagnostic.
