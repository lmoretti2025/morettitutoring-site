Why now

The test is required again.

More than 60 colleges and university systems have reinstated a testing requirement for the 2027 cycle, including six of the eight Ivy League universities, with Columbia joining by 2027. Families who spent four years hearing “test-optional” now have a score to plan for — and junior year is the window to do it in.

On AI

In one study, students who used ChatGPT for homework scored 48% better on practice problems and 17% worse on a closed-book test. AI explains concepts well. It does not build the recall a student needs under time pressure, which is exactly what the practice work in this program is built to develop.

How the program works

No two plans look the same. The standard behind them does.

There is no fixed weekly syllabus, and that is deliberate — the order and the pace are set by what your student’s diagnostic finds, not by a schedule written before we met them. What doesn’t move is everything on the right.

Built for your student

Set by the diagnostic

  • Which skills we work on — and in what order. Points recoverable, not syllabus order.
  • How long each one takes. Some close in a session; some take three.
  • When full-length tests fall — placed where they will actually tell us something.
  • How much homework, matched to how far beyond the syllabus your student wants to go.
  • How the plan changes as gaps close and new ones surface.

The Moretti Method

The thinking behind every plan.

01

Evaluate

A full-length, timed, adaptive diagnostic shows which skills cost points and where the time goes.

02

Design

A session-by-session plan sequenced around what that test found, and around a target date chosen together.

03

Practice

Deliberate repetition on the skills that move the score, plus the pacing habits that protect points already earned.

04

Adapt

Every phase ends in a test and a written report, so the plan adjusts on evidence rather than on impression.
