How I tutor

Most math tutoring expires at the end of the hour.

Reviewing tonight’s assignment and hoping it sticks by tomorrow’s quiz is not a plan. The visible problem is this week’s chapter. The real one is usually a year or two back: fractions under the quadratics, exponent rules under the logs.

So the first sessions find where the ladder actually broke, and the plan fixes that first. Because I teach every course from Algebra 1 through Multivariable, the person who fixes the gap is the person who teaches what depends on it.

The plan

Same grade, two different plans.

What fills the first eight weeks is decided by where your student’s recent tests and homework show the gaps are. The first weeks often go somewhere the syllabus has already left behind, and the chapter your student is graded on gets easier because the thing underneath it finally holds.

  • Found from their own work. Recent tests, quizzes and homework, read for where the points went.
  • Built on their course. Their textbook, their teacher’s exams, in the order that moves the grade fastest.
  • Checked every eight weeks. A written report on what improved, what is next and how the grade is tracking.

Questions

What parents usually ask.

Do you meet in person or online?
Both. Online sessions run anywhere in the country, using the same materials and the same portal as in-person students. In-person sessions are held at the Cranford Public Library in Cranford, New Jersey. Most families outside New Jersey work with us entirely online.
What math courses do you cover?
Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2/Trigonometry, Precalculus, Statistics and AP Statistics, AP Calculus AB & BC, Multivariable Calculus, and IB Mathematics (AA and AI) at SL and HL.
How is this different from generic homework help?
Homework help treats the symptom. We find the gap underneath it, and the plan fixes that first, so the new material finally sticks instead of sitting on top of a shaky foundation.
Is this only for students who are struggling?
No. Strong students use the program to get ahead of the classroom's pace and stay there. The plan comes from where the student actually is, behind or ahead.
Who does the teaching?
Luca. Every session, every week. There are no handoffs to junior tutors and no rotating staff. I write the plan and I sit with your student every week.
How long is each session?
Sessions run 60–90 minutes depending on the course and where your student is, scheduled weekly or twice a week at a consistent time.
Is there a minimum commitment?
No. Sessions are billed by the hour, individually or from a prepaid pack, and you can stop whenever you like; the terms explain how unused pack hours are refunded. Most families keep going through the school year, since math builds on itself, and every eight weeks you get a written report on where things stand, so the decision to continue is an informed one.
How much does tutoring cost?
Families come to me with very different arrangements: one or two sessions a week, a single hard course or a year-long catch-up, a student getting ahead rather than catching up, siblings together. One posted number would be wrong for most of them, so after our first conversation I give you a recommended plan and the cost in writing, before the first session. Sessions are billed by the hour, individually or from a prepaid pack, and you are never committed beyond what you have paid for. Packs are prepaid hours that stay good for six months, and hours still unused after that expire. If tutoring ends early, ask for a refund before then: the unused hours come back, and the hours already used are charged at the single-session rate. The full payment terms, including the late fee, are in the terms. The first call costs nothing either way.
