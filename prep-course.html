How the work runs

From the first conversation to test day.

  1. A free first conversation

    Send a recent Bluebook, PSAT or SAT score and we talk it through, or your student takes the free diagnostic first. Either way you leave knowing where the points are going and roughly how many sessions it would take.

  2. A plan built from the numbers

    The report ranks every skill by how many points it is costing and how teachable it is. The sessions follow that order, and you have the plan and the total cost in writing before the first one.

  3. One pattern at a time

    Your student explains each step, then works harder versions of the question they just got right, until the method is fast and holds up on questions they have never seen. Some patterns take thirty minutes. Some take several sessions.

  4. Between sessions

    Practice assigned in the portal from the skills we worked on, a weekly email to you on what we covered and what comes next, quick questions by message, and an open online office hour every week.

  5. Full-length checkpoints

    Timed practice tests at the points where the result will tell us something. The portal separates real progress from a good or bad morning, so a sixty-point swing is never mistaken for a trend.

  6. The final weeks

    The work shifts from learning to performing: pacing, which questions to leave and come back to, and the decisions that protect points on test day. No new rules at the last minute.

Is it the right fit?

When one-on-one tutoring makes sense.

The score has stopped moving.

Your student has studied and taken practice tests, and the score keeps landing in the same range. Something specific is holding it there, and it rarely shows on a score report.

The target is 1500 or higher.

Near the top of the scale every question counts for more, and the last points come from timing and habits as much as content.

There is one attempt left.

With applications close, there is no room for a sitting that goes sideways. The plan has to aim at the one date that counts.

A group class covers the syllabus, which helps when the syllabus is the problem. It cannot watch how your student reads a question, where the time goes, or the moment they drop a method that was working. One-on-one sessions can. And if a class, practicing on their own, or a later test date would serve your student better, I will tell you on the call.

Ask a question
Do you meet in person or online?
Online. Most students work with me online, from anywhere in the country, using the same materials and the same portal as in-person students.

In person. Available in Cranford, New Jersey. In-person sessions are held exclusively at the Cranford Public Library.

The materials, the method and the instructor are the same either way, and many families mix the two.
How long is each session?
Sessions run about an hour, scheduled weekly at a consistent time. In the final weeks before a test date we can extend or add sessions as the plan requires.
When should my student start preparing?
It depends on the distance between where your student is and where they want to be. Sharpening timing and one weak section can take a handful of sessions. Rebuilding algebra or reading from a standing start does better with three months or more. For most juniors that means beginning in the fall or early winter for spring test dates, and on our first call I will give you a realistic number for your student.
Which test date should we target?
The first official sitting that leaves room for one retake before application deadlines. We map this out together at the start, so the whole season is planned from the beginning.
Who does the teaching?
Luca. Every session, every week. There are no handoffs to junior tutors and no rotating staff. I build the plan and I teach it.
What if we miss a week or a session?
Tell me in advance and we reschedule within the same week where possible. The plan pauses and picks up where it left off, so nothing in it is lost, and you only pay for sessions that happen.
Is sophomore year too early?
For full SAT preparation, usually yes. Junior year is the sweet spot. But sophomores with gaps in algebra or reading benefit a great deal from course tutoring first, so SAT prep later starts from strength.
How much does tutoring cost?
Sessions are billed individually or in a pack, and how many your student needs depends on where they start and what they are aiming for: a few sessions before a retake, a full season from a standing start, Math only, two siblings at once. One posted number would be wrong for most families, so after our first conversation I give you a recommended number of sessions and the total cost in writing, before anything is scheduled. The call and the diagnostic cost nothing either way.
